# AGENTS.md

Context for whoever maintains welcome-home next, person or agent. [docs/features.md](docs/features.md) describes each feature in detail, [docs/user-guide.md](docs/user-guide.md) is for owners, [docs/security.md](docs/security.md) and [docs/deployment.md](docs/deployment.md) cover data and the stack, and [docs/runbook.md](docs/runbook.md) production operations; this file is the map: what the app is for, how it is built and why, how it is tested, and where it could go next.

## What it is

A private web app where a few owners connect their Nuki account and create date-bound links that open their door, and their building's entrance if a Nuki Opener opens it: a guest opens the link on their phone and slides to open, with no app, account or code.

**Roles**

- **Owner**: connects Nuki by authorizing welcome-home in Nuki (OAuth2) or with an API token, imports locks, creates, edits, regenerates and revokes links, reads each lock's history.
- **Admin**: an owner who can also invite owners, list them, create passkey recovery links, list and cancel both kinds of link, and delete owners. Admins never see other owners' locks, links or history.
- **Guest**: no account; has only a link.

**In production**: a Dockge stack on a private server, reached only through its own Cloudflare Tunnel. Image `ghcr.io/jfisbein/welcome-home`, at the major version's tag (`:v2`). The repository is public, so its public URL, its host and how to reach it are not here (Conventions): `https://welcome-home.example.com` stands for the URL, and the owner has the rest.

## Requirements

Functional:

- Registration only by single-use invitation (72 h); sign-in only with passkeys, no passwords. The first admin comes from a command line invitation (`--bootstrap-admin`).
- An owner connects Nuki by authorizing welcome-home in Nuki (OAuth2), or by pasting a Nuki Web API token. Either is checked against Nuki before it is stored, encrypted; an OAuth2 access token is refreshed before it expires. A refresh it refuses, or a 401 that it repeats when asked once more, marks the connection `DISCONNECTED`; a daily job checks every connection, and an hourly one tries those disconnected in the last 7 days, connecting them again if Nuki accepts them.
- Locks are imported from Nuki on connecting it, when the owner asks and every day, smart locks and Nuki Openers alike; each has a name, a time zone (from Nuki where possible) and can be deactivated when it leaves the Nuki account. A smart lock may have one of the owner's Openers as its building entrance: its links then open both, the entrance first.
- A link has a label and a validity window entered in the lock's time zone and stored in UTC. Its state (pending, active, expired, revoked) is computed, never stored. It may be limited to certain days of the week with hours for each, one range per day, local to the lock.
- A guest's GET never acts on the lock: only a POST after sliding to open unlatches (link previews in WhatsApp, Telegram or Slack make GETs). The guest learns whether the door opened, failed, or was refused in night mode.
- Every attempt with a valid link is recorded with IP address and country; attempts are purged after a retention (365 days), their IP addresses after a shorter one (90 days), and the guest page says so.
- Owners who lose their passkey get a recovery link (admin panel or `--recover-owner`); they keep their other passkeys.
- Owners get emails about their locks (opened, failed, denied, Nuki disconnected, a lock's problems) and choose which ones, once they have verified their address with the link sent when they register.
- Spanish, English, French, German and Italian: a guest's pages in their browser's language, an owner's pages and emails in the language they chose.

Non-functional:

- Owners are isolated from each other in the database itself (Row-Level Security), not only in code.
- Secrets at rest: Nuki tokens are encrypted (AEAD, bound to their owner and kind); links store no token at all (HMAC).
- Never two unlatches for one press: one action per lock at a time, and the unlatch is never retried once Nuki may have run it.
- Single instance: rate limits, the per-lock mutex and scheduled jobs live in memory in one process.
- GDPR: guests' IP addresses expire after 90 days, their browsers are not kept, guests are told on their page, and so does a link a year after it ended, with the guest's name in its label; a deleted owner's data is gone from backups after their 30-day retention.
- Upgrades within a major version are pulling the new image: no backup, no change on the server, and rolling back is pinning the previous release.

Out of scope so far: geofencing, push notifications, use limits, exceptions to a link's schedule (holidays), Ring to Open, native apps.

## Architecture

A modular monolith: one Spring Boot app is the only component that talks to Nuki, Postgres and the secrets. Modules are the packages under `welcomehome`, checked by `ModularityTests` (Spring Modulith): a module uses another only through its public types.

| Module   | Responsibility                                                                                              |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `auth`   | Owners' accounts and how they sign in: security configuration, passkey sign-in, invitations and recovery links (`InvitationService`), registration from one (`OwnerRegistration`), the owner's passkeys page (`PasskeysController`), the owner's account page (`AccountController`: changing their email, closing their account), deleting an owner (`OwnerAccounts`, which publishes `OwnerDeleting`), whether the menu offers the admin panel (`NavigationModel`), `CurrentOwner` |
| `admin`  | The admin panel and the `--bootstrap-admin` and `--recover-owner` commands, through `auth`'s `InvitationService` and `OwnerAccounts`; it depends on `auth`, never the reverse, so its controller gets `CurrentOwner` like the others |
| `preferences` | An owner's preferences (`PreferencesController`): the language of their pages and emails, in `owner`, and each browser's color theme, in the `theme` cookie (`Theme`, given to every page by `ThemeModel`); the language every page is drawn in (`OwnerLanguageResolver`) |
| `config` | `WelcomeHomeProperties`: the public URL (`BASE_URL`), checked once at startup, from which every module builds its links; `Languages`: the languages with a bundle; `TimeZoneNames`: a time zone's name in a language, which pages and emails show instead of its id; depends on no other module |
| `crypto` | Keysets (`Keysets`, `KeysetGenerator`, `KeysetRotator`), `CryptoService` (AEAD), `LinkTokenSigner` (MAC)  |
| `db`     | `OwnerTransactions`: runs work in a transaction with `app.owner_id` set for Row-Level Security             |
| `nuki`   | The only module that talks to Nuki: `LockProvider` (interface), `NukiWebLockProvider`, `NukiWebClient` (the API's calls), `NukiRequests` (timeouts, retries, what a failure means), `NukiCodes` (Nuki's codes as types), `NukiOAuthClient` (authorizing and tokens), connections (`NukiConnections`: an API token or OAuth2 tokens, refreshed before they expire) and their daily check, connecting with OAuth2 (`NukiAuthorizationController`), Nuki's webhooks reporting the outcome of an action (`NukiWebhookController`, `ActionReports`) |
| `locks`  | Import (`LockSync`), settings and state of each owner's locks, smart locks and Openers, a lock's entrance, and what is wrong with them for the pages that list them (`LockChecks`) |
| `links`  | Access links: create, list, edit, regenerate, revoke (`AccessLinks`, `LinkStatus`, `LinkUrls`), and the days and hours at which they open (`LinkSchedule`, `LinkSchedules`) |
| `access` | The guest flow: guest page, `POST /api/open` (`OpenDoor`), rate limits, client IP, per-lock mutex (`LockMutex`), unlatch confirmation |
| `audit`  | Access events, history pages, the home page's latest attempts (`RecentAttempts`), both drawn from `AttemptRow`s (`AttemptRows`: time in the lock's zone, outcome and country in the reader's language), purge job; publishes `AccessAttempted` |
| `welcomehome` (root package, not a module) | What spans modules: the home page (`HomeController`), `/settings` and the former addresses of its sections (`SettingsController`), the error pages (`ErrorPages`) |
| `notifications` | Emails to owners (opened, failed, denied, Nuki disconnected, a lock's problems) by their preferences, and always about their account (recovery link created, passkey added, and to the previous address when the owner changes it), sent after commit (`OwnerNotifier`) |

**Guest flow** (`access.OpenDoor`): the order of its checks, and how an opening is confirmed, are in [docs/features.md](docs/features.md#guest-page), with a diagram. What must not change: a GET never acts on the lock, the MAC is checked before anything is read in the owner's context, and one action runs per lock at a time.

**`LockProvider`** (`findConnection`, `listLocks`, `getSnapshot`, `listSnapshots`, `unlatch`, `getRecentLog`, all by owner id) isolates Nuki: tokens are decrypted inside `nuki` and never leave it. Another way to reach a lock (Nuki Bridge, MQTT) is another implementation or a change inside `nuki`, without touching the rest.

**Words.** A *lock* is a device in Nuki, a smart lock or an Opener: `NukiDevice` as Nuki reports it, `OwnerLock` as an owner keeps it. A *door* is what a guest opens, and the *entrance* the building's door that an Opener opens; both are locks to the code. Of a lock, `LockSnapshot` is all that Nuki last knew, and `LockPosition` where it stands within that. Of an action, `LockLogEntry` is its line in the lock's log, `LoggedAction` what it was, `ActionResult` how it ended, and `PendingAction` one Nuki accepted and has yet to run. `LockMutex` lets one run per lock. An *attempt* is a guest trying a link: an `AccessEvent` when recorded, an `access_event` row once stored, an `AttemptRow` when shown, and its `AccessOutcome` how it ended for the guest. An owner's pages say "Estado" for much of this: the UI's words and the bundles' keys stay as they are.

**Data model** (`src/main/resources/db/changelog/changes/`): `owner` (`email`, `language`, and the email's verification: `email_verified_at`, the link's hash and expiry; the only columns `app_user` may update), `invitation` (also recovery links: `recovers_owner_id`), `nuki_connection` (`credential`: `API_TOKEN` or `OAUTH2`; `access_token_encrypted`, and for OAuth2 `refresh_token_encrypted` and `access_token_expires_at`), `lock` (`device_type`: `SMART_LOCK` or `OPENER`; `entrance_lock_id`, the Opener at a smart lock's building entrance), `access_link` (`token_version`), `access_link_schedule` (a link's days and hours: `day_of_week`, `opens_at`, `closes_at`, local to the lock; no rows, any time), `access_event` (`ip`, `country`, and `entrance_lock_id` for an attempt at the entrance; `lock_id` is always the link's lock), `notification_preference`, and Spring Security's `user_entities` and `user_credentials`. Every table with `owner_id` has Row-Level Security with a policy that fails closed (`owner_id = nullif(current_setting('app.owner_id', true), '')::uuid`). `owner` and `invitation` have no `owner_id` and no RLS: only `auth` uses `invitation`, and other modules read `owner` only where the table below says. Nor do the passkey tables, which Spring Security reads to learn who is signing in. They are tied to their owner by foreign keys that cascade: `user_entities.owner_id`, which a trigger fills in from `name`, the owner's email, as Spring Security writes it without one, and `user_credentials.user_entity_user_id`. `auth` joins `user_entities` by `owner_id` in every query.

**Tables by module.** Spring Modulith checks only Java dependencies; SQL crosses modules in the places below, all reads for display or a daily job, except `preferences` saving an owner's language. Anything else goes through the owning module's public types.

| Table | Owner | Also used in SQL by |
|-------|-------|---------------------|
| `owner` | `auth` (registration inserts it) | `admin`: lists owners; `preferences`: the owner's language, read for every page and saved; `audit`, `links`, `locks`, `nuki`: owner ids for their daily jobs; `notifications`: the owner's email |
| `user_entities`, `user_credentials` | `auth` (Spring Security's passkey store) | none (deleting an owner cascades to them) |
| `invitation` | `auth` | none (`admin` goes through `InvitationService`) |
| `nuki_connection` | `nuki` | none |
| `lock` | `locks` | `links`: lock names in link lists; `audit`: names and time zones in the history and the home page's attempts; `notifications`: name and time zone; `resolve_link` |
| `access_link` | `links` | `audit`: labels in the history; `notifications`: labels; `access`: only through `resolve_link` |
| `access_link_schedule` | `links` | none (`access` goes through `LinkSchedules`) |
| `access_event` | `audit` | none |
| `notification_preference` | `notifications` | none |

**Roles in Postgres**: `migrator` owns the schema and runs Liquibase; the app connects as `app_user`, with no ownership and no `BYPASSRLS`; the `backup` service and the off-site backup tool read as `backup_reader`, with `BYPASSRLS` and `pg_read_all_data` and no other grant. No `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, so that `resolve_link` (owned by `migrator`) sees the link. Roles come from `deploy/db-init/01-create-roles.sh`, used by development, tests and production alike.

**Jobs** (`@Scheduled`, server time zone): Nuki connection check (08:00), trying disconnected connections again (hourly), locks' sync and the check of their problems (08:15), access event purge (03:30), purge of links that ended over a year ago (03:32), purge of invitations expired more than 30 days ago (03:35); in production a separate `backup` container dumps the database at 04:00.

**Commands**: `--bootstrap-admin` and `--recover-owner=<email>` start the app without the web server (`WelcomeHomeApplication.runsACommand`), print a link and exit. Anything web-only must stay optional without a web server (see `CommandLineTests`).

## Decisions and why

| Decision | Why |
|----------|-----|
| OAuth2 with Nuki offered first, a pasted API token folded away as the alternative; OAuth2 off without a client id and secret | Nothing to generate or paste, and access renews itself. Nuki grants the client only with its Advanced API (granted to welcome-home on 2026-10-25), so an install without one still works with tokens. |
| The OAuth2 client written in `nuki` (`NukiAuthorizationController`, `NukiOAuthClient`), not Spring Security's | Spring's stores tokens in clear, and its token client would need customizing for Nuki anyway; what it would save, the `state`, the PKCE verifier and the redirect, is a few lines, covered by `NukiAuthorizationTests`. |
| OAuth2 tokens in `nuki_connection`, encrypted like an API token; one refresh at a time per owner, 5 minutes before expiry; a token request never repeated once Nuki may have answered | Nuki rotates the refresh token: two refreshes with one, or a repeated one, would leave a dead token. A refresh failure is never `NO_ANSWER`, which would tell a guest that the door may have opened. |
| 2.0.0 renamed `api_token_encrypted` to `access_token_encrypted` | Nobody but the owner used the app yet; the changeset has a `<rollback>`, checked once both ways, and the dump taken before upgrading restores 1.x. |
| A 401 disconnects only if a read repeats it, and a disconnection is tried again hourly for 7 days | Disconnecting stops every link of the owner, so a passing mistake of Nuki's must not do it, nor last until the owner notices. Credentials stay stored while disconnected, which makes trying them cheap; 7 days bounds the requests for an access truly revoked. The owner is emailed both ways, under the same preference. |
| Disconnecting Nuki or deleting an owner revokes an OAuth2 authorization at Nuki, best effort | Deleting the tokens here leaves them valid at Nuki, with encrypted copies in dumps for 30 days. Revoking the refresh token ends its access tokens too (RFC 7009). A failure is logged and changes nothing else, as the owner asked to leave. The endpoint is Nuki's `revocation_endpoint`, at `oauth.nuki.io`; a request there without credentials answers 400, and at `api.nuki.io`, 404. Connecting again does not revoke the tokens it replaces. |
| Sessions end when what let them in is gone: deleting a passkey ends the owner's other sessions, deleting an owner all of theirs | A lost phone must not stay signed in, and a deleted admin must not keep their role, until the session expires. `OwnerSessions` keeps them in memory, as Tomcat does, so a restart loses nothing it needs. Spring's `SessionRegistry` was not used: it hears of sessions through a session strategy, which `WebAuthnConfigurer` does not set. Nor does it set the strategy that changes the session id on signing in, so `SecurityConfiguration` does. |
| Unlatch (action 3), confirmed from the lock's log | Nuki runs actions asynchronously: a 204 only means accepted. |
| Owners connected with OAuth2 unlatch through Nuki's Advanced API, whose webhook reports the outcome; the log is read meanwhile, and whichever tells first counts | A guest learns at once whether the door opened, rather than when the log shows it. The log stays because the webhook is not sure to come: Nuki documents no retries, turns a client's webhooks off after too many failures, and sends none for an API token, which is the owner's own. Only reports of actions are used, matched in memory by their request id (single instance), so no owner is looked up without an owner context. The `webhook.central` scope is asked for but not required: Nuki's central webhooks, the only ones that report the Advanced API's actions, need it; a client whose Advanced API has no webhooks may not ask for it, so on `invalid_scope` the owner is sent to Nuki once more without it. A refused advanced request (4xx other than 429) ran nothing, so the plain action follows; one Nuki may have run is never repeated. The webhook's credential is Nuki's signature with the client secret, so it has no CSRF token or session. |
| A Nuki Opener is a lock of another type in the same `LockProvider`, and a smart lock may name one as its building entrance, whose slide its links' guest page shows first | Nuki's Web API lists Openers among its smartlocks and opens them with the same action, so no adapter of its own; and a guest who must pass a building's entrance and then a door needs both from one link (the user's choice, 2026-10-27, over separate links per device). `access_event.lock_id` stays the link's lock, the Opener going in `entrance_lock_id`, so link histories, emails' history links and the previous release keep reading events as before. Nuki does not document how an Opener logs an opening: it is taken to be a lock's unlatch, and an unconfirmed opening logs what the log showed. |
| A refusal at Nuki's token endpoint disconnects an owner only if it is about their code or tokens | A 401, `invalid_client` or `unauthorized_client` is about welcome-home's client, such as a wrong or rotated secret: disconnecting would hit every OAuth2 owner at the next daily check, and correcting the secret would not bring them back. It logs an error and counts as Nuki failing. |
| The unlatch is retried only on 429 or when no connection was made | Retrying after Nuki may have run it could open the door twice. |
| A lock's time zone shown by its name, never its id; Spain's zones named by the bundles; a new lock imported with the server's zone when Nuki's keeps the same time | An id such as `Europe/Berlin` is technical, and wrong for a lock in Madrid, which Nuki reports so as it has one zone per region. Java names Madrid "hora de Europa central", so the bundles name Spain's two zones ("hora de España peninsular", "hora de Canarias"), where the owners are (the user's choice, 2026-11-03); others keep Java's name. Import takes the server's zone when Nuki's has the same offset now and at every change over ten years, as owners are most likely where the server is: a lock in Paris then comes in as `Europe/Madrid`, which its owner can change. Syncs never change the zone of a lock already imported, so an owner whose lock came in as `Europe/Berlin` changes it once. The settings list ids too, each with its name, as zones that share a name must be told apart there. |
| Guests' IP addresses forgotten after 90 days, attempts kept 365; the browser not kept; the guest page says so | The owner sees where an attempt came from in its country, which stays; an IP address is what identifies the guest, and 90 days covers looking into a recent misuse (the user's choice, 2026-11-04). The browser was stored and never shown. The job sets the two columns to null rather than deleting, so the history keeps its rows; `app_user` may update only them, and the release before reads a null IP as one not kept. Not a legal opinion: an owner with paying guests may need one. |
| A link's schedule: per day, one range a day, in `access_link_schedule`, apart from `LinkStatus` | Nuki Web's one range for all the chosen days cannot say "Monday to Friday 9 to 17, Saturday 10 to 14" without two links, and so two URLs for a guest (the user's choice, 2026-11-02, for one range per day; `opens_at` in the key leaves room for more). Local times, so 09:00 stays 09:00 when the clocks change. A closing time not after the opening time is on the next day, and a "Todo el día" tick per row, stored as 00:00 to 00:00, is the whole day: the time 00:00 to 00:00 alone was not evident (the user's remark, 2026-11-02). A link's state still says only its dates, as the schedule closes and opens every day. Nuki knows nothing of it: every opening goes through the app. `resolve_link` does not return it, as changing what a function returns takes a `DROP`; the MAC is verified first, so the owner's context can read it. **A rollback to a release before 2.6.0 ignores schedules**, so such links open at any time within their dates: the user accepted this, with a warning in the runbook, as the alternative is a new major version. |
| Passkeys only, invitations only, and passkeys must verify their user | No passwords to leak; a private app with few, known users. A passkey is the only factor, so registering and signing in require user verification (a PIN, fingerprint or face): a security key without a PIN, which anyone holding it could touch, is refused. Phones, computers and password managers verify anyway. |
| Links are `{link_id}.{HMAC(link_id, token_version)}` | Nothing to steal from the database; a URL can be shown again; incrementing `token_version` invalidates every earlier URL. |
| Row-Level Security plus `owner_id` in every query | Isolation does not depend on every query being right. `OwnerIsolationTests` checks every route with an id. |
| AEAD with the owner id as associated data | A ciphertext copied to another owner's row does not decrypt. |
| Admins see only emails and dates | An admin is still just an owner to RLS. Recovery links are the exception the user chose: whoever holds one signs in as that owner, so the admin who creates one could. The owner is emailed when one is created and when a passkey is added, so its use is not hidden from them. |
| An owner changes their email at once, and the new address is verified like a new owner's; the previous one, if verified, is told; their other sessions end | The email is not a credential, as signing in takes a passkey: it is where notices go and the name passkeys know the owner by, so a typo at registration must be fixable without the old address. Notices stop until the new one is verified, as for a new owner. Telling the previous address keeps a change made from someone else's hands from going unnoticed. The other sessions still know the owner by the previous email, so they end; the one that asked goes on under the new one. Passkeys on the owner's devices keep showing the previous email: nothing tells them, and they still sign in. |
| An owner closes their own account, unless they are the only admin | The admin panel did it for them before, so a mistyped email meant asking an admin. The only admin cannot, as an admin must always be left, as in the admin panel. |
| Recovery adds a passkey and keeps the old ones | The user's choice; the owner then deletes the lost device's passkey at `/settings/passkeys`, which never deletes the last one. |
| `CF-Connecting-IP` for the guest's IP, `forward-headers-strategy=framework` | The tunnel is the only way in, so Cloudflare's headers are trustworthy; `X-Forwarded-For` starts with whatever the client sent. |
| `/o/{token}` masked in logs, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `noindex` | Guest URLs are bearer credentials. |
| No CSRF token on `POST /api/open`, and no session for guests | The link's token in the body is the credential, which a forged request would need anyway; a CSRF token kept in the session went stale when a guest opened more than 30 minutes after loading the page. |
| No ports published; own cloudflared in the stack | With a published port the app was reachable from the LAN and the forwarded headers could be spoofed. |
| Off-site dumps by Databasus over `dbbackup`, an internal network that only `db` and Databasus join, as `backup_reader` (`BYPASSRLS`, `pg_read_all_data`) | No published port, so the database stays out of the LAN and keeps no way out, and the tool reaches neither `app` nor the internet through it. A role that writes nothing rather than the superuser, which could also run programs (`COPY ... TO PROGRAM`). Created by `db-init`, not Liquibase: making a `BYPASSRLS` role takes a superuser, and dumps carry its `CONNECT` grant, so it must exist before `restore.sh` runs on a new server, when a restored changelog would say that the changeset already ran. |
| Database passwords as files (`configtree:`), keysets as Docker secrets | No secrets in environment variables or in the image. |
| Keysets backed up apart from the dumps (Vaultwarden) | A stolen dump alone does not expose the Nuki tokens. |
| Releases are `vX.Y.Z` tags; production follows the major version's tag (`:v2`) | Upgrading is Dockge's Update, with no backup and no change on the server. The running release is the image's `org.opencontainers.image.version` label; rolling back is pinning the previous one. It holds because releases within a major are backward compatible (Conventions). |
| Emails are sent after commit, on another thread, and dropped if they fail | A guest never waits for SMTP, and a mail problem never fails an opening. At most one email about refused attempts per link per hour. |
| Guests slide to open (a native `input type="range"`), not press a button | A touch by mistake, in a pocket or while scrolling, must not open the door. A native range, so keyboards and screen readers work it; `open.js` undoes a jump to where the track was touched and moves a quarter per key. After an opening it works again a minute later, its thumb back at the start after 2 seconds but off until then, so that a touch by mistake with the page still open does not open again; after a failure at once; after expired, revoked or invalid never. |
| Configuration's pages under `/settings/...`, their former addresses redirecting (301) | Emails already sent link to `/nuki/connection`, `/notifications` and `/passkeys`: within a major version, every URL already sent keeps working. |
| The app's own error page; `/error` public; the session only in its cookie | Spring Boot's page broke the app's look, and a guest's error on a public route was redirected to /login, starting a session whose id Tomcat put in the URL (`;jsessionid=`). An unknown address still sends a visitor who is not signed in to /login, so no route shows it exists. |
| The color theme in a cookie, not in the owner's row | The user's choice: it belongs to the browser, so a phone and a laptop may differ, and it also applies to the pages before signing in. The server writes `<html data-theme>`, so no script and no flash of the other theme; no cookie means the system's. |
| Pico CSS 2.1.1 kept, though Pico is unmaintained since 2.1.1 (repositories archived) | It is a static CSS file served from `static/css/` with no JavaScript, so there are no fixes to miss, and what it lacks goes in `app.css`. The templates lean on its classless styling of semantic HTML (`article`, `nav`, `fieldset role="group"`, `aria-busy`, `aria-current`, automatic dark mode): Bootstrap, Bulma or Pure would mean adding classes to every template for no visible gain, and Pure is barely maintained either. Revisit if a browser starts drawing something wrong that `app.css` cannot fix cheaply, or for a redesign; then prefer a fork of Pico, or own CSS keeping its variables. |

## Conventions

- **Language**: code, comments, javadoc, docs and commit messages in English. Table, column and enum names in English. UI texts only in `i18n/messages*.properties` (Spanish the default bundle; English, French, German and Italian the others): never in Java, templates or JavaScript, which `TemplateTextTests` checks. A message with markup goes through `th:utext`, with every value escaped by `#strings.escapeXml`; an apostrophe in a message is written `''`. A new language: "Adding a language" in `docs/features.md`.
- **Javadoc** on every public class and method (`-Xdoclint:all -Werror` fails `check`). Comment the why, sparingly.
- **Nullness**: everything is non-null unless `@Nullable` (JSpecify); every package has `@NullMarked`; NullAway fails the build. Spring 7's `JdbcClient.query(X.class).list()` returns `List<@Nullable X>`: use `new SimplePropertyRowMapper<>(X.class)` or a lambda for lists.
- **Compatibility**: within a major version every release is backward compatible, as production pulls `:vN` without reading release notes and rolls back by pinning the previous release. So: the previous release runs against the new schema; no new required property, secret or environment variable (a new one has a default); nothing to change in `deploy/`; every URL already sent keeps working (guest links, invitations, recovery and verification links, history URLs). Migrations only expand the schema: new tables, nullable columns or columns with a default, indexes, grants; never drop, rename or retype, never add NOT NULL or UNIQUE to what exists, never revoke, every changeset in a transaction. `MigrationCompatibilityTests` checks the changesets from `COMPATIBLE_FROM` on. Anything else waits for the next major, which moves `COMPATIBLE_FROM` past its breaking changesets and production to `:vN+1`.
- **Liquibase**: XML with native tags; `<sql>` only for CHECK constraints, RLS, GRANTs and functions. A new table with `owner_id` gets `ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, its policy and its grants to `app_user` in the same changeset (`MigrationTests` fails otherwise).
- **Data access**: `JdbcClient` with records, only in classes named `*Dao`, which `DataAccessTests` checks; owner data only inside `OwnerTransactions.execute/run`, with `owner_id` in the query as well. No `findById(id)` without an owner.
- **Configuration**: `application.yaml` (and `application-dev.yaml`); `@ConfigurationProperties` records; durations in ISO-8601 (`PT1M`).
- **Web**: Thymeleaf templates, Pico CSS classes; every page's `<html>` carries `th:lang="${#locale.language}"` and `th:attr="data-theme=${theme}"`; every signed-in page starts with `fragments/layout :: menu('<section>')` (home, locks, settings or admin), and configuration's pages add `settings('<section>')` (nuki, preferences, notifications, passkeys or account); guests' attempts are drawn by `fragments/attempts :: table(attempts, withLock)`, on the home page and in the history alike; a lock's time zone is shown by its name, `${@timeZoneNames.of(zone, #locale)}` ("hora de Europa central"), never its id, except where the owner chooses one; a page that must start no session sets `sessionless`, so the layout leaves out the CSRF meta tags. Two Thymeleaf traps: a fragment selector also matches elements by name, so no fragment is named like an element (hence `menu`, not `nav`); and `th:replace` runs before `th:if` on the same element, so a conditional fragment goes inside `<th:block th:if>`. The CSP also forbids inline styles: scripts change styles only through the DOM (`element.style`), never `<style>` or `style=` in markup; CSRF on every form, HTML forms send PUT/PATCH/DELETE through `_method`. JS in `static/js/`, one small file per behavior, no inline scripts (CSP `script-src 'self'`); a script's texts come from its page, as hidden lists or `data-*` attributes. Routes taking an id must be covered by `OwnerIsolationTests` (a guard test lists them).
- **What stays out of the repository**, which is public, history included: production's hostnames, addresses, container ids and paths on the server, and when anyone opened a door or which devices they have. Tokens and keysets, of course. What runs where belongs in the owner's private notes, what each release changed in its GitHub release.
- **Commits**: one per task, pushed to `main` once `./gradlew build` is green.

## Testing strategy

`./gradlew build` compiles (Error Prone, NullAway), runs every test and checks javadoc. Tests start from rows written by `TestData` (owners, locks, links, schedules, attempts, connections, passkeys), so that a change to a table means a change there; a test writes its own SQL only when the SQL is what it checks, such as Row-Level Security or a constraint. Most tests are integration tests (`@IntegrationTest`): a real Postgres in Testcontainers, initialised with the production role script, migrated by Liquibase as `migrator`, used by the app as `app_user`, with freshly generated keysets.

- **Nuki**: never the real API in tests. `NukiStandInConfiguration` starts WireMock in place of `api.nuki.io`; `NukiWebClientTests` covers status codes, timeouts, retries and backoff; `NukiOAuthClientTests` where an owner authorizes, the token endpoint and revoking; `NukiAuthorizationTests` the way to Nuki and every way back; `NukiTokenRefreshTests` refreshing (once for simultaneous calls, disconnecting when refused, not when Nuki refuses the client, never `NO_ANSWER`); `NukiConnectionWithoutAuthorizationTests` the app without an OAuth2 client; `NukiWebhookTests` Nuki's webhooks: the signature (checked against Nuki's own example), reports matched by request id whichever arrives first, error codes, and what is refused or ignored.
- **Passkeys**: `PasskeyEmulator` (webauthn4j-test) answers registration and sign-in like a browser with an authenticator, so the real WebAuthn endpoints are exercised end to end.
- **Sessions**: `PasskeyManagementTests` (deleting a passkey ends the other sessions, signing in changes the session id), `AdminPanelTests` (a deleted owner's session ends).
- **Isolation**: `OwnerIsolationTests` (every id route answers 404 for another owner and changes nothing), `MigrationTests` (every `owner_id` table has RLS and its policy), `RowLevelSecurityTests` (what `app_user` sees with and without an owner context), `ModularityTests` (module boundaries), `AdminPanelTests` (admins see no locks or links).
- **Guest flow**: `OpenDoorTests` (order of checks, results, confirmation from the log or Nuki's webhook, the Advanced API's fallback to the plain action, concurrency, client IP, the building entrance), `LockMutexTests` (one action per lock), `TokenLoggingTests` (tokens never logged), `GuestPageTests` (a GET never calls the lock provider; one slider per door; a link's schedule; what sliding keeps).
- **History's retention**: `EventPurgeJobTests` (attempts deleted after a year, IP addresses forgotten after 90 days, browsers from earlier releases too, and that `app_user` may update nothing else of an attempt).
- **Links' schedules**: `LinkScheduleTests` (hours, midnight, the clocks changing, the first opening), `LinkScheduleFormTests` (the form, what is refused, a schedule said in each language).
- **Pages**: `HomePageTests` (locks, latest attempts, the same rows as the history, the menu and `/settings`), `LockWarningsTests` (what is wrong with a lock on the home page and `/locks`, and a Nuki that fails or is slow), `ThemeTests` (the theme cookie and `data-theme` on every page), `TimeZoneNamesTests` (a lock's zone named alike on every page, ids only in its settings), `SignedInErrorPageTests` (the error page with the menu, through MockMvc and `/error`). What MockMvc does not show runs against a real server (`@IntegrationTest(webEnvironment = RANDOM_PORT)`): `ErrorPagesTests` (errors forwarded to `/error`, no session for guests, JSON for scripts) and `SessionCookieTests` (cookie flags, the session never in a URL).
- **JavaScript**: `src/test/java/welcomehome/browser` runs it in headless Chromium, which Playwright downloads when first used, on a real server with the Nuki stand-in, with pages of a phone's size. `GuestSlideTests` slides `open.js` with real touch events (what keeps a touch by mistake from opening, keys, what follows each outcome); `OwnerPagesTests` registers and signs in through `webauthn.js` with Chromium's virtual authenticator, and tries `confirm.js` and `schedule.js`. CI installs the browser's system packages first (`./gradlew installBrowser`).
- **Backups**: `.github/scripts/test_backup_restore.sh`, in CI, runs production's stack, `backup.sh` and `restore.sh` on throwaway secrets and test data.
- **Commands**: `CommandLineTests` starts the app without the web server.
- **Emails**: `MailStandInConfiguration` starts GreenMail as the SMTP server; `NotificationTests` checks each kind of email, preferences, the per-link quiet period, the owner's language and that two simultaneous rejections send one email; `AccountSecurityEmailTests` the recovery and passkey emails; `EmailVerificationTests` the link that verifies an owner's address, and that nothing else goes to it before.
- **Languages**: `MessageBundlesTests` (bundles have the same keys and each message the same arguments, apostrophes are doubled, templates name only existing ones), `PagesInEveryLanguageTests` (every page in every language, no missing text), `TemplateTextTests` (no text in templates, escaped `th:utext`), `OwnerLanguageTests` (an owner's language, and that `app_user` may update no other column of `owner`).
- **Practice**: start with `git fetch`: the development clone has been dozens of commits behind `origin/main`, which is where work from other machines and release merges land. Each change is checked with a mutation test by hand (break the code, see a test fail, restore it), and deployment changes are tried on a local copy of the production stack (`COMPOSE_PROJECT_NAME=...` to stay apart from the development database).
- **By hand in a browser**, for what no test runs: how pages look, in light and dark and at phone width, and the scripts with no test of their own (`copy.js`, `share.js`). Run the dev app (`docker compose up -d`, `./gradlew bootRun`) and use Chrome; owner pages need the owner's passkey, which only the owner can use (sessions live in memory, so a restart signs them out). Sample rows go into the development database as its superuser (`docker compose exec -T db psql -U postgres -d welcome_home`) and are deleted afterwards. For the guest page to offer opening without any risk, add a `nuki_connection` with status `OK` and `access_token_encrypted` set to `'\x00'`: the token does not decrypt, so an opening fails as unavailable before anything reaches Nuki. Never with a real token. Chrome's window does not shrink below about 616 px: for phone widths the owner switches on DevTools' device mode, and sizes are read with JavaScript (`innerWidth`, `scrollWidth`).

Never call Nuki's unlatch for real outside a guest sliding to open: it opens a real door. Development uses a real Nuki token only for read-only calls.

## Build, run, release

```sh
./gradlew build                   # everything CI runs
./gradlew generateKeysets         # development keysets in secrets/ (git-ignored)
docker compose up -d              # development Postgres on localhost:5432
./gradlew bootRun                 # the app on http://localhost:8080 with the dev profile
./gradlew rotateAeadKeyset --args=<file>
```

`.github/workflows/image.yml`: pull requests build, test and build the image without pushing; pushes to `main` publish `edge`; tags `vX.Y.Z` publish `vX.Y.Z`, `vX` and `latest` (prereleases only their own tag), and then create the GitHub release.

Release: a pull request named `Version X.Y.Z` from branch `release-X.Y.Z` that bumps `version` in `build.gradle.kts`; once merged, tag the merge commit `vX.Y.Z` and push the tag. Its description becomes the GitHub release's notes, so it is written for whoever deploys: what changed since the last release, and anything to do before deploying. The workflow drops its first paragraph when it starts with "Sets the project's version", and Claude Code's attribution (`.github/scripts/release_notes.py`). Production follows `:v2`, so `deploy/compose.yaml` changes only for a new major version. Upgrading production and everything else on the server is in the runbook.

**Nuki's OAuth2, as checked by hand on 2026-10-25** with read-only scopes (Nuki's current guide is docs.nuki.io/guide/authorization/oauth2; older guides and forum posts describe outdated endpoints):

- Nuki granted welcome-home's Advanced API on 2026-10-25, of a type without webhooks or advanced actions (see above); the owner deleted it and applied again on 2026-10-28, as "Smart Home", which Nuki's guide (docs.nuki.io/guide/overview) lists with OAuth2 and webhooks ("Short Rental" needs a Smart Hosting subscription per lock), with a 500-character description, and Nuki granted it that day with the same client id and secret. Its tab in Nuki Web shows the client id and secret and lists the callback URLs, `https://welcome-home.example.com/nuki/callback,http://localhost:8080/nuki/callback`. Production keeps them in the stack's `secrets/nuki-oauth-client-id` and `secrets/nuki-oauth-client-secret`; development reads them from `secrets/config/welcomehome.nuki.oauth.client-id` and `secrets/config/welcomehome.nuki.oauth.client-secret`.
- Nuki's authorization server is ORY Hydra at `oauth.nuki.io` (seen 2026-10-26): `GET https://api.nuki.io/oauth/authorize` answers 303 to `https://oauth.nuki.io/oauth2/auth` with the query unchanged, and Hydra's discovery document, `https://oauth.nuki.io/.well-known/openid-configuration`, lists `code_challenge_methods_supported` `plain` and `S256`, and a `revocation_endpoint` (`https://oauth.nuki.io/oauth2/revoke`). A malformed `code_challenge` or an unknown method still reaches Nuki's sign-in page: Hydra checks them only after the owner consents, and the verifier at `POST /oauth/token`. The app sends PKCE (`code_challenge` S256 to `/oauth/authorize`, `code_verifier` to `/oauth/token`) since 2026-10-26; that day the owner connected with it in development (`OAUTH2`, `OK`, with a refresh token). Whether Nuki refuses a wrong verifier was not tried.
- `GET /oauth/authorize` sends the owner back with `code`, `scope` and the `state` whole; `POST /oauth/token` with the client's id and secret both as HTTP Basic and in the form answers `token_type` `bearer`, `expires_in` 3599, `scope`, `access_token` and, with `offline_access`, `refresh_token`; `grant_type=refresh_token` answers new access and refresh tokens. The refresh token rotates on every refresh; whether the previous one still works was not checked, so the app assumes it does not. Nuki's guide says refresh tokens last 90 days and that, if the access token is not refreshed within them, the owner must authorize again; whether each refresh starts a new 90 days or they count from the authorization, it does not say. The daily check refreshes every owner, so with the first reading no owner ever has to connect again; with the second, every owner would every 90 days (and get the disconnection email). The owner's production connection, made on 2026-10-26, tells on 2027-01-24. The tokens are opaque, not JWTs, so they do not tell either.
- Nuki's rate limits (docs.nuki.io/guide/endpoints/rate-limits): 4 requests per second, 100 per minute, 1000 per hour, then 429 until the window passes; the page does not say whether per token, account or client. One guest's opening (an unlatch and up to about 8 reads of the log) takes more than a day's refreshes; the daily check, 2 requests per owner in a row, would only near 100 per minute at about 50 owners, if the limit is per client.
- Nuki's consent page ("A welcome-home le gustaría:") lists each scope with Nuki Web's labels for API tokens, seen with all four on 2026-10-26: in Spanish, `smartlock.log` "Ver el protocolo de cierre y recibir notificaciones de registro", `smartlock.readOnly` "Ver dispositivos", `offline_access` "Mantener el acceso a los datos hasta su revocación" (its description says the owner can revoke it at any time), and `smartlock.action` "Gestionar dispositivos" ("Se permite operar dispositivos Nuki"). Then "Cancelar" and "Autorizar"; "Cancelar" sends the owner back with `error=access_denied` and the `state`, which the app tells apart (seen 2026-10-26). Its "No me preguntes de nuevo" remembers the consent; asking for more scopes asks again.

**Other ideas**, roughly by value:

- Owners who cannot use a passkey. First learn why: today almost every device can hold one (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, Bitwarden or 1Password), a computer without one can use the phone's by scanning a QR code, and a security key costs little; help on the invitation page, by platform, may be all it takes. If a real alternative is needed, a one-time code by email: the owner asks for it with their email, gets 6 digits valid for 10 minutes, and types them in the same browser, which a mail scanner opening a link could not use up, and which ties it to the browser that asked. Only to verified addresses, single use, kept as a hash like the verification link, rate limited by email and IP, and an email to the owner on each sign-in this way. Spring Security has one-time token login (`oneTimeTokenLogin()`), but its JDBC store keeps tokens in clear. It makes the email account the owner's credential, weaker than a passkey: so offer it per owner, or only until they add a passkey, and record the trade-off in Decisions. Passwords would add a secret to leak and still need resetting by email, and TOTP alone is a weak factor: neither is worth it here.
- Re-encrypting stored Nuki tokens after an AEAD rotation, so that old keys can be dropped without owners connecting again.
- Links with a maximum number of uses (an `access_link` column).
- Geofence as a deterrent (browsers accept fake locations: store only distance and accuracy, and tell the guest first).
- A local Nuki Bridge or MQTT adapter as another `LockProvider`.
- Cloudflare Access in front of the owner pages, excluding `/o/*` and `/api/open`.
