# Code review, 2026-10-19

A full read of welcome-home at `0b09dc5` (v1.4.0): build, modules, migrations, templates, JavaScript, tests, deploy stack and runbook. The build was not run; everything below comes from reading.

## Verdict

For a weekend project this is well above the usual bar. The big decisions are right and are written down with their reasons: Row-Level Security plus `owner_id` in every query, links with no secret in the database (HMAC over `link_id` and `token_version`), AEAD bound to the owner, a GET that never acts on the lock, one unlatch per lock at a time, confirmation from the lock's log, isolation tests with a route guard, a stack that publishes no port. The findings below are what I would change, in order of importance.

## Findings, by priority

### 1. `OwnerTransactions` joins the surrounding transaction, and `set_config` lasts to the end of it

`db/OwnerTransactions.java` uses `TransactionTemplate` with the default propagation (`REQUIRED`) and `set_config('app.owner_id', ..., true)`. Inside an outer `@Transactional` method, `execute(ownerA)` followed by `execute(ownerB)` leaves the context on B for the rest of the outer transaction, silently. It does not happen today (no `@Transactional` method calls `OwnerTransactions` with a different owner), but it is exactly the kind of trap RLS exists to rule out.

Fix: `PROPAGATION_REQUIRES_NEW`, or read `current_setting('app.owner_id', true)` on entry and fail when an owner is already set and differs.

### 2. A read timeout on the unlatch POST becomes `nuki-error` and re-enables the button

In `access/OpenDoor.unlatchAndConfirm`, a `LockProviderException` with reason `UNAVAILABLE` returns `OpenResult.NUKI_ERROR` without looking at the log. A read timeout after the request was sent means Nuki may have run the action; the guest page then invites a retry ("Inténtalo de nuevo en unos segundos"), which is a second unlatch.

Fix: on a timeout of the unlatch request, fall through to `UnlatchConfirmation.await` anyway. The log says whether the door opened, and the "never two unlatches" rule then also holds against the human retry.

### 3. An admin can sign in as any owner through a recovery link

Documented as a decision, but now that the app sends email the opacity is cheap to remove: email the owner when a recovery link is created for them, when one is redeemed, and when a passkey is added to their account (by recovery or from `/passkeys`). The admin keeps the power; the owner learns about its use.

### 4. Outcome codes are strings spread over four places

`opened`, `nuki-error`, `unavailable` and the rest live in `access/OpenResult.code`, `audit/AccessHistoryController.OUTCOMES`, `notifications/NotificationKind.of` and the `FAILURES` and `DENIALS` maps of `notifications/OwnerNotifier`. A typo passes silently through `getOrDefault`. There is already one inconsistency: `UNAVAILABLE` is `AccessResult.DENIED` in `access` and `NotificationKind.FAILED` in `notifications`.

Fix: a public enum in `audit` (say `AccessOutcome`) that `access` produces and the other modules consume, so the compiler checks every `switch`.

### 5. Two `UNAVAILABLE`s with opposite meanings

`LockProviderException.Reason.UNAVAILABLE` means "Nuki did not answer"; `OpenResult.UNAVAILABLE` means "the door is not available, the owner must act". `OpenDoor` maps one to the other crosswise (`Reason.UNAVAILABLE` becomes `OpenResult.NUKI_ERROR`, everything else `OpenResult.UNAVAILABLE`). Rename one: `Reason.NUKI_UNREACHABLE` or `OpenResult.DOOR_UNAVAILABLE`.

### 6. Connecting verifies only the read scope

`NukiConnections.connect` checks the token with `GET /smartlock`, which proves `smartlock.readOnly` only. A token without `smartlock.log` fails the first time a guest opens, with a 403 that does not disconnect and that the owner never sees; the guest gets "unavailable". At connect time, also read the log of one lock (`/smartlock/{id}/log?limit=1`). The action scope cannot be checked without acting, but a `MISSING_PERMISSION` should at least show on the owner's panel and connection page, not only as "not available" to the guest.

### 7. Request threads block in `Thread.sleep`

`nuki/NukiWebClient` (backoff) and `access/UnlatchConfirmation` (up to 15 s per opening) sleep on Tomcat threads. On Java 25, `spring.threads.virtual.enabled=true` in `application.yaml` solves it in one line and also moves the `@Async` listeners to virtual threads.

### 8. `MaxRAMPercentage=75` with no memory limit in the stack

The JVM sizes its heap against the whole LXC. Add `mem_limit` (or `deploy.resources.limits.memory`) to the `app` service in `deploy/compose.yaml`.

### 9. No injectable `Clock`

Ten `Instant.now()` and `LocalDateTime.now()` calls across `OpenDoor`, `GuestController`, `AccessLinks`, `LinkForm`, `UnlatchConfirmation`, `EventPurgeJob` and others. A `Clock` bean would make the tests of `LinkStatus`, confirmation, purge and the denied quiet period deterministic; today they depend on the wall clock and on `now()` in SQL.

### 10. `BASE_URL` is parsed in four places

`admin/InvitationService`, `links/LinkUrls`, `notifications/OwnerNotifier` (each strips the trailing slash) and `auth/SecurityConfiguration` (host and origin). Only `InvitationService` checks that it is not blank. One `@ConfigurationProperties("welcomehome")` record with a `URI baseUrl`, validated once, that the others inject.

## Schema and naming

- **`access_event.nuki_response jsonb` is never written.** `audit/AccessEvents.record` does not insert it. Drop it or fill it.
- **`lock.connection_id` is write-only.** `LockDao.upsert` sets it; nothing reads it, and `GuestLinks.doorAvailable` checks `nuki_connection.status` by owner. Either drop it (and with it `nuki_connection.id`, keying the table by `owner_id` like `notification_preference`) or use it for availability.
- **`lock` and `owner` are Postgres keywords** (non-reserved, so it works). `smart_lock` or `door_lock` avoids `LOCK TABLE lock` and trouble on any other engine.
- **No way to promote or demote an admin** once registered: `is_admin` is set only when an invitation is redeemed, and `app_user` has no `UPDATE` on `owner`. A functional gap, not a bug.
- **Expired invitations are never purged** (`app_user` has no `DELETE` on `invitation`). Add them to the purge job.
- **The history cursor is `occurred_at` alone.** Two events in the same microsecond lose one when paging. Cursor on `(occurred_at, id)`.
- **`last_checked_at::timestamp` and Spring Security's `timestamp` columns** depend on the JDBC session's time zone. Return `OffsetDateTime` and convert in the view.
- **Nine shapes of "link"**: `ResolvedLink`, `GuestLink`, `EditableLink`, `StoredLink`, `RegeneratedLink`, `RevokedLink`, `InsertedLink`, `LinkView`, `RegeneratedUrl`. One canonical `AccessLink` record plus two or three projections would do.
- **Names.** `LinkController` and `LinksController` differ by one letter (`LockLinksController` says what the second one is). `LockActions` does not say it is a mutex; it is the singleflight pattern (`PerLockMutex`). `OwnerAccountDao` (`auth`) and `OwnerDao` (`admin`) are two DAOs on the same table with the same `findByEmail` in two modules. `CryptoService` encrypts one thing (`ApiTokenCipher`).
- **Modules are isolated in Java but not in SQL.** `notifications` reads `access_link`, `lock` and `owner`; `audit` joins `access_link`; `nuki` and `audit` read `owner`; `admin` deletes from `user_credentials` and `user_entities`. Spring Modulith cannot see any of it. Either accept it and document which module owns each table, or route those reads through public APIs.

## Minor

- Spanish UI strings inside Java (`AccessHistoryController.OUTCOMES`, `OwnerNotifier.FAILURES` and `DENIALS`, email subjects, the test email). A `messages.properties` with `MessageSource` takes them out of the code and ties them to the enum of finding 4.
- The daily connection check could also sync locks; today a lock removed in Nuki is only noticed when the owner presses "Sincronizar".
- In `UnlatchConfirmation`, filter the log by `trigger` or by the token's name, so that an unlatch the owner did from the Nuki app in the same 5 s window is not attributed to the guest.
- CI compiles twice (Gradle on the runner, then again inside the `Dockerfile`), and the `--mount=type=cache` for Gradle is not kept in the GHA cache, so every image build downloads the dependencies again.
- The javadoc of `HomeController` ("locks and links come later") is stale.
- Every guest GET creates a session for the session-based CSRF token, link-preview bots included. Negligible in size; `CookieCsrfTokenRepository` would avoid it.
- Tests: very good behavioural coverage (278, almost all integration tests against a real Postgres, WireMock, GreenMail and a passkey emulator). The weak points are the real clock and the random IPs and UUIDs, which the `Clock` of finding 9 would partly fix.
