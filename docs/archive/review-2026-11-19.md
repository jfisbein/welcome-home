# Critical review, 2026-11-19

> **Archived.** A record of the review of v2.17.0 and of what became of each of its items up to 4.2.0 (2026-11-21), kept for its reasoning: 103 fixed, 12 discarded, 2 put off, which [status](../dev/status.md) carries. Do not take it as a list of work to do. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) says how things are now.

A full review of welcome-home at `6f86ea1` (v2.17.0): architecture, database, code, simplicity, security, tests, operations, and everything a guest, an owner and an admin see. It is a list of work to choose from, each item with its state, kept up to date as the work goes: when an item is done or ruled out, its state changes here in the same task, and so does the count under State of the work.

## How it was done

- Five reviews in parallel, each reading its whole area: the domain modules (`access`, `links`, `locks`, `audit`, `notifications`, `preferences`), the security modules (`auth`, `admin`, `nuki`, `crypto`, `config`, `db`), the database with the build and `deploy/`, the tests, and the user's side (templates, scripts, the five bundles, the user guide).
- The tests were run in a clean clone with JaCoCo attached, and one mutation was tried (see Numbers). The user's side was looked at in real screenshots at 375 and 1280 px against the Nuki stand-in.
- Three passes of my own on top: every feature for a simpler way to do it, the whole path to an unlatch read as an attacker, and the guest page and the owner's defaults read as someone who does not understand the app.
- Checked again by me against the code: every SEC, MIS and COR item rated High, and COR-01 to COR-04. The other items carry their reviewer's file and line and were not checked twice. An item marked *suspected* depends on something that reading cannot settle (Nuki's behaviour, the server's configuration).
- Nothing was changed, and nothing reached Nuki.

**Rated for what the app is**: five friends, real doors. Scale and load count for nothing. Whatever can open a door for the wrong person, or for the right person at the wrong place, counts in full, as does what costs upkeep without earning it at this size.

## Verdict

The foundations are right and should not be touched: links with no secret in the database, Row-Level Security with `owner_id` in every query, a GET that never acts, the unlatch never repeated once Nuki may have run it, passkeys with user verification, a container that publishes no port. No way was found for a stranger without a link to open a door or read another owner's data.

The weak points are around them:

1. **A door can be opened from anywhere by mistake**, with the defaults: the location check is off unless an owner turns it on, a new link is valid from now, and where the check is on, a laptop's vague position or a refused permission gets past it (MIS-01 to MIS-08).
2. **The PIN does less than it promises**: a 4-digit one falls to guessing within a booking, and the page shows the instructions (the address, where a key is) before asking for it (SEC-01 to SEC-03).
3. **When something breaks, nobody is told** (OPS-01), and an opening can leave no trace in the history (COR-02).
4. **The app has grown past its size**: two ways to unlatch, two ways to connect, three location modes, a state machine for one warning, five languages. Each is sound alone; together they are most of what a maintainer must hold in mind (SIM-01 to SIM-07).

## Legend

- **Severity**: Critical (a door opens for someone who should not, or data crosses owners, with no precondition), High (the same with a realistic precondition, or a guest left outside with nobody told), Medium (worth fixing; a nuisance or a latent trap), Low (polish).
- **Effort**: S under 2 hours, M half a day to a day, L several days.
- **State**: **Pending**; **Fixed**, with the release that carries it, or "after" the last release while it waits for one; or **Discarded**, with the reason and the date, for an item the owner of the project decided not to do. A part of an item that was ruled out is said in its text, and the item stays Pending for the rest.

No item is Critical.

## What is solid, and should stay as it is

- **Links** as `id.HMAC(id, token_version)`, resolved by a function that needs no owner, so the database holds nothing to steal and a URL can be shown again or invalidated.
- **Owners kept apart** in the database itself: policies that fail closed, `owner_id` in every query and in the foreign keys, a transaction that refuses a second owner, and tests that find every route with an id by themselves.
- **The unlatch** at Nuki's boundary: repeated only when Nuki cannot have run it, and an unanswered one sent to the log rather than back to the guest.
- **Signing in**: passkeys that must verify their user, tokens of 256 bits kept as hashes and used once, sessions that end with what let them in.
- **The guest's page**: a GET that never acts, a slider that a keyboard and a screen reader can work, words that never mention Nuki, tokens or unlatching.
- **The stack**: no published port, a read-only file system, no capabilities, secrets as files, and a restore of production's own stack run in CI.
- **The texts' discipline**: one name per thing, kept by tests in five languages.
